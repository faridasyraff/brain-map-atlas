# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primarily students and people learning or exploring neuroscience. Researchers remain served (precise data, coordinates, shareable views), but beginners come first: nobody has to understand advanced terminology before they can explore.

- Students explore where brain regions are, what they do and how they relate, for coursework, exams or curiosity, often with no specific goal.
- Researchers find a region fast, check its position, and share a precise view with others.

## Product Purpose

Brain Atlas is a web atlas of the Allen Mouse Brain Common Coordinate Framework v3 (CCFv3). It shows the brain in three linked section planes and as a 3D model. Clicking any point names the region under it and moves every other view to that point. Success means a user can go from "what is this?" or "where is X?" to a confident answer in a few seconds, and can come back to it later.

## Positioning

Every view is linked to one point in the real atlas volume: a click in any 2D section or on a 3D slice plane resolves through the annotation to a named region, and all other views follow. Search covers single structures and whole anatomical groups, and the AI assistant answers about whatever is selected.

## Operating Context

- Used at a desk on a laptop or desktop, and on phones for quick lookups.
- Sessions alternate between scrubbing through sections, clicking to identify, searching by name or acronym, and reading about a region.
- Links are shared between people (the URL encodes the selected region and all three slice positions).
- The backend is a Flask API in Docker serving slices, lookups, meshes, the ontology, accounts and the assistant.

## Capabilities and Constraints

All of these stay first-class in any redesign:

- Three linked 2D section views (sagittal, coronal, transverse) with click-to-identify, slice stepping, zoom and pan.
- 3D model: slice planes that follow the sections, the selected region's mesh (falling back to the nearest parent mesh, labelled as a substitute), and eBrain mode (click a slice plane in 3D to identify a region).
- Search across single structures and groups; the full Allen hierarchy tree.
- Region details: hierarchy path, summary, keywords, related regions.
- AI assistant about the selected region (local Ollama model or OpenAI GPT).
- Accounts: saved regions with private notes, recently viewed, account deletion.
- Shareable links that restore the region and slice positions.
- Phone layout.

On opening the atlas, a user sees and can use the views right away, as today: the sections and 3D model are on screen and clickable, with no intro step in the way.

Terminology: "region", "section", "slice", "sagittal / coronal / transverse", "3D". The brain is a mouse brain.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Brain Atlas.
- No AI-tool attribution anywhere in the product or repository.

## Evidence on Hand

- Real atlas data through the running API: section images (`/api/slice`), region lookups, highlights, meshes (`/meshes/<id>.glb`), the ontology, region centres and volumes.
- AI summaries are empty when no OpenAI key is configured; the product says so rather than showing invented text.
- No testimonials, user counts or institutional endorsements exist; none may be invented. Atlas data is credited to the Allen Institute for Brain Science.

## Learning model

Every region is explained at two levels:

- Level 1, simple: one or two sentences a student understands immediately (e.g. the hippocampus "helps your brain form certain memories and find its way around places").
- Level 2, technical: anatomy, atlas metadata (identifiers, hierarchy, coordinates, volume), connections, related structures and references, opened on request.

Exploration questions the product must make effortless: what is that, what is next to it, what does it connect to, what happens if I isolate it, where is it, why does it matter. Return loops are natural, not gamified: recently explored, saved structures, continue exploring, related and suggested structures, connections, interesting facts, comparing two structures.

Open: where Level 1 text comes from at scale (authored, or generated by the assistant and labelled) and the source of connection data; the current API has related regions and AI summaries only.

## Product Principles

1. One point, every view: anything that identifies a region moves all views to it.
2. Show the real atlas: real sections, real meshes, real names; never decorative stand-ins for data.
3. Nothing is removed to simplify: complexity is revealed progressively, never cut.
4. Fast to an answer: identifying or finding a region takes seconds, on desktop and phone.
5. Simple first, technical on request: every screen reads for a beginner and opens up for an expert.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard access to every control, visible focus, WCAG AA text contrast, labelled icon buttons, and respect for reduced-motion preferences.
